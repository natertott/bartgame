# Independent source-art critique — forest second pass

Reviewed 2026-10-31. This is a **source-art review**, not an emulator capture or a gameplay verdict. The gallery manifest identifies `e5e3fcb21fe1bcc50c965943164146a2dc1e3c46`; every file in its `source_sha256` map matched the current file at review time. I inspected the native-size and integer-enlarged drawings in the boards below. No screenshot was recolored, retouched, or treated as implemented evidence.

Read the shared art bible, direction log and reference register before review. The six previously read tutorial notes remain the project's reference basis; this review does not claim another reading of their original PDFs or new research.

## Overall finding

The new infection is visibly stronger than the previous forest study: black exposed trunks and attached saturated violet structures replace leaf-covered brown trees. The sick fields remain olive/gray rather than becoming an all-purple floor. This preserves a useful value and hue distinction between traversable earth, infected vertical forms, brass interactions and pale actors. I found no source-art issue that requires blocking the five alternatives from user comparison.

There are still meaningful selection tradeoffs. The natural fields are deliberately spare, and the healthy oak/birch/fir group labels share essentially the same crown construction within a treatment. The sets therefore read as one reusable vegetation family rather than many distinct species. The source assemblies also expose straight tile rhythms in large pools and repeated canopy masses. Explicit corners improve joins without making an arbitrary generated outline fully organic.

The source boards likewise do not establish foreground occlusion or playable clearance.

## Interactions and height

The single large brass padlock with a black keyhole is the clearest interaction cue in the set. Its common drawing across all five treatments is useful: the meaning stays stable while surroundings change. The bomb stone's branching pale fracture reads differently from the whole, rounded lift boulder. The five webs share pale fibrous material but visibly differ in radial, woven and sagging construction.

The pressure switches are broad, simple faces with recessed down states. Option 3's wooden face is quieter than the pale round/diamond faces; it will rely more on its frame and physical depression against warm dirt. Unlit censers also remain lower contrast than their lit states. Those are actual-scene review priorities, not failures demonstrated by these isolated boards.

The ten wooden chest designs have recognizable lids, front latches and genuinely dark open cavities. The barrel-like, basket-like, stump and damaged forms vary more substantially than recolored rectangles. Their low-value wood/brass palette is deliberately less conspicuous than the taller golden relic markers. The narrow rectangular shrine in option 2 can initially resemble a sign or mailbox; the flame/available state and paired ground pad carry the healing-font meaning.

The paired fallen log has one continuous bark mass, an exposed cut end and a near-side/contact shadow. Its source drawing promises a solid obstruction, not a crawl-under passage. Ledges use a green top lip over a darker visible stone face. Collision, orphan-pair cleanup, underpass height and after-clearing floor restoration require the separate native/host/runtime evidence.

The isolated-water three-floor board has no opaque green rectangular exterior on sick turf or dirt. Its tiny pool remains a bounded blue cavity. Larger water examples have connected pale rims and explicit inner corners; their square long sides still retain grid geometry.

## Folk and portraits

The snail's shell/stalks, morel cap, owl face and teapot spout are distinct even at world scale. The gardener family retains the watering/tool role across beetle, frog, carrot, salamander and mole. Portraits repeat those identity details rather than introducing a different character. The updated willow has separated hanging boughs and gaps; the salamander's elongated jaw and curled tail improve its animal read. Ragdoll, flower and bell sisters are particularly different from the old hood-only family.

The friendly Moth Sister and selected Moth Confessor still share pale split wings and antennae. I compared the friendly board with the selected enemy's `knight-all-frames-source.png`: the Sister's round bright face, compact stationary silhouette and unarmed book/hand mass differ from the Confessor's long legs, dark narrow face, stance changes and separate blade. This reduces confusion in source form, but their family resemblance remains a deliberate actual-context tradeoff. No new animation set is implied for these static folk drawings.
