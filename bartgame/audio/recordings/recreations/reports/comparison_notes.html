What the two versions contain

R01–R08 · Original mix on GBA

The uploaded performance, instruments, singing, and speech remain in the audio wherever present. The mix is resampled offline, given one common linear headroom gain, and converted to signed 8-bit stereo PCM for the GBA.

This is the closest performance comparison: it preserves the source recording’s arrangement and timing while exposing the GBA conversion and output characteristics.

I01–I08 · Instrument reconstruction

Automatically detected notes and percussion transients are played with fresh FluidR3 General MIDI sounds. The renderer receives symbolic score events; no uploaded waveform, source vocal, or extracted audio stem is mixed into these versions.

Detected vocal pitches can become instrumental lead notes. Singing, spoken words, original timbres, and exact performance details are not recreated.

The instrumental versions are automatic approximations, not note-perfect transcriptions. They follow the complete source timeline, but mixed-track pitch recognition can miss notes, confuse octaves, or assign a voice pitch to the wrong instrument. Full duration does not establish complete musical accuracy.

Each file covers the original decoded duration, including introductions, fades, video lead-ins, and silent tails. No time stretch, musical cut, new tempo grid, or extra three-second loop preview is added. Tiny cartridge frame-alignment padding is outside the requested review length. Capture startup and emulator resampler latency remain untouched.

How the instrumental versions were rebuilt

Basic Pitch ran locally on full-duration analysis views of each mix. Harmonic/percussive decomposition separates signal characteristics; it does not isolate singers or individual instruments. The selected harmonic view supplies nonbass candidates, while a bass-band pass supplies bass candidates. A model-based vocal/instrument stem-separation pilot was explored, but it was not the full-song reconstruction input.

A salience-weighted foreground path, counter line, harmonic support, and monophonic bass are assigned to new GM sounds by heuristic roles. Bass pitch-bend trajectories are retained, with an independent low-band pitch estimate used only to help choose among existing candidates. Percussion candidates come from low/mid/high spectral-flux transients, without filling in a metronome or inventing missing beats.

These choices have practical limits: vocal consonants, bass attacks, and plucks can trigger false drum hits; overlapping or quiet drums can disappear; dense harmony and unpitched speech can confuse note detection. Model salience is not a calibrated correctness score. The MIDI’s 120 BPM setting is only a seconds-to-beats export timebase, not a claim about the source tempo or a retiming of the music.

The saved score/MIDI events, input hashes, model provenance, and structural validation are included with the project. They make the reconstruction reviewable and editable; they do not certify transcription accuracy.

What the GBA playback changes

The player streams left and right PCM planes from ROM through DMA1/FIFO A and DMA2/FIFO B, clocked by Timer0. It performs no runtime sample mixing, decompression, floating-point DSP, or per-sample interrupt, and needs no software sample buffer. Both original conversions and instrument versions are precombined before gameplay.

The 65,536 Hz mode synchronizes the sample and PWM clocks; 32,768 Hz uses its matching clock mode. Hardware probes showed substantial re-clocking spurs at intermediate unsynchronized rates, so a nominal 44.15 kHz option was not selected just to reduce ROM size. Higher rates do not turn 8-bit PCM into hi-fi source precision.

Quantization uses nearest-step signed 8-bit conversion with triangular dither, while exact digital silence is kept at zero. The downloadable FLAC files preserve the captured 44.1 kHz/16-bit stereo samples losslessly; MP3 copies are lossy listening conveniences. That capture container does not increase the underlying cartridge precision. These are emulator recordings, not physical GBA speaker or headphone-jack recordings.

Understanding the source-comparison measurements

Raw waveform differences are sensitive to bass phase and output filtering. For bass-heavy R01, the direct, gain-fitted comparison reported 9.34 dB SNR. Modeling mGBA’s expected blip-buffer high-pass response, with an approximately 13.7 Hz corner, raised that diagnostic comparison to 31.59 dB and correlation 0.99965. R08 showed the same general pattern.

This was an analysis-only filter model applied to the reference comparison. It did not alter the exported PCM or captured WAV. The 0.99965 value describes that R01 diagnostic; it is not a global reconstruction accuracy percentage or a rating of all sixteen tracks. See the raw comparison results and coloration diagnostic.

Verification and resource cost

All 16 full-length captures passed digital integrity checks, with zero clipped samples. ROM and audio hashes, exact sample counts, emulator version, clock settings, and output-level measurements are recorded in the evidence. These checks verify the playback files and implementation, not whether every reconstructed note matches the source.

The measured driver object uses 2,052 bytes of code/constants and 20 bytes of static state, excluding stack, hardware FIFO/register state, the player UI, and the rest of a game. Across the sixteen captures, the driver call averaged 0.07121–0.07157% of a video frame; its largest observed update was 0.3293%.

Continuous DMA bus use outside those calls was measured separately. At 65,536 Hz stereo, a paired arithmetic-loop fixture measured 0.994% throughput loss for code running in ROM and 1.015% in IWRAM. These emulator fixture results are not universal gameplay ceilings or proof of an absolute optimum. The main tradeoff is storage: the largest delivered R ROM is 31,168,800 bytes, and the largest I ROM is 16,805,856 bytes.

The source-derived recordings, editable note data and comparison files are published in this repository at the user’s request. Compiled ROMs remain separate chat-download artifacts under the repository’s existing ignore policy.

Listening review

Use R to judge how the original performance survives the GBA output path. Use I to judge the newly rendered instrumental version: the main hook, bass register and bends, drum timing, and recognizable section changes. Differences in the I versions should be treated as transcription or orchestration choices to review, rather than evidence that the original mix was preserved exactly.
